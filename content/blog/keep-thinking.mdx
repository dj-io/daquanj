I used to think the scarce thing was output. Pages, tickets, answers. Models made that cheaper. What they did not make cheaper is *judgment* — the part where you decide the answer is wrong, or incomplete, or aimed at the wrong question.

That is the part I want to protect.

A good tool shortens the distance between a question and a first draft. A bad tool shortens the distance between a question and a finished-looking paragraph you did not actually think. The second one is flattering. It is also how you get fluent and dull at the same time.

I am not interested in refusing the tools. I use them. I build them. The discipline is to keep a private standard for when something is done:

> I could defend this to a skeptical friend without the model in the room.

Lifelong learning, for me, is not a feed of new frameworks. It is the habit of staying with a problem after the first plausible answer arrives. The first answer is usually the model's. The interesting one shows up later, when you have sat with the residue.

Keep thinking. The rest is logistics.
