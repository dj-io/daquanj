*Motions that keep you inside the argument.*

Code editors took vim seriously because people who write all day will not give up their hands. Writing tools mostly did not. You get a blinking cursor, a mouse, and a hope that "markdown mode" counts as craft.

I wanted the same muscle memory in a document that I have in a buffer: words as objects, sentences as ranges, motions that compose. ProseMirror is a strong foundation for that. What it did not have — for years, and loudly — was a drop-in vim layer.

[@prose-motions/core](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@prose-motions/core) is my answer to that request. Not a clone of every vim edge case. A motions library you can put on a ProseMirror editor without rebuilding the editor.

## Why motions matter in prose

In code, vim is navigation. In prose, it is also *thinking*. When you can jump by sentence, yank a clause, and put it two paragraphs up without leaving the keyboard, you stay inside the argument. The mouse is a context switch. Enough of those and the sentence goes cold.

Researchers and writers deserve that. So do people building agent workspaces, where the human still has to edit what the model drafted.

## Small library, long request

The useful lesson was not technical. A two-year community request is a map. Someone already did the product discovery. Your job is to ship the missing piece at the right altitude — reusable, boring to integrate, and maintained after the announcement.

I sidelined heavier product work around it. The library is still the part I stand behind. If you write in a ProseMirror surface and your hands expect `w`, `ciw`, and friends, that is what it is for.
